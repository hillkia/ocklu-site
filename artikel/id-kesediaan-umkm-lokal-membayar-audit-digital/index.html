The enforcement of digital accessibility regulations has shifted the operational landscape for commercial platforms operating within the European Union. In Germany, this European directive has been transposed into domestic legislation through the Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG) [6]. Rather than remaining a theoretical standard, market surveillance and legal enforcement have accelerated significantly; since August 2025, competitors and consumer protection associations have actively issued warning letters, while newly established oversight bodies scrutinize digital compliance [5, 13].

Within this regulatory environment, the primary target market comprises mid-sized German e-commerce enterprises that exceed specific operational thresholds. Specifically, the legislation applies to private-sector commercial entities operating online shops, booking systems, or digital customer portals unless they qualify for statutory exemptions [5, 22]. The law explicitly excludes micro-enterprises defined as having fewer than 10 employees and an annual turnover or balance sheet total below €2 million [5]. Consequently, commercial online retailers with an annual turnover exceeding €2 million face direct legal liability under the BFSG [5].
