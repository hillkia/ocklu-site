Regulatory Misalignment and Sectoral Priorities

The demand for universal web accessibility standards, such as those that would accommodate users with disabilities, directly clashes with the operational realities and compliance frameworks currently governing commercial entities. In the Indonesian digital and regulatory landscape, statutory enforcement heavily prioritizes national security, content control, and administrative compliance over user interface accessibility. For instance, regulatory bodies such as the Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs (Komdigi) focus their enforcement mechanisms on enforcing registration mandates for private electronic service operators, occasionally blocking major infrastructure like Wikimedia authentication domains for non-compliance [1].

When examining specific sectors like commercial banking, legal obligations are similarly directed away from Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) compliance. Instead, institutional frameworks in banking and financial governance are structurally bound to anti-money laundering protocols, cybersecurity mandates, and systemic risk mitigation, often aligned with international bodies like the Financial Action Task Force. Because current statutory obligations and enforcement priorities center on data protection, transaction security, and state-monitored internet filtering—exemplified by the Information and Electronic Transactions (ITE) Law—accessibility standards for web platforms remain legally unmandated and secondary to core operational security requirements [1]. Consequently, disability advocates face a profound regulatory vacuum, as existing sector-specific frameworks lack any statutory mechanism to compel banking institutions to adopt universal design principles.
