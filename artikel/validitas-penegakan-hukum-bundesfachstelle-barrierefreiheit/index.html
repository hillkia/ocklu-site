Introduction to the Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz and Enforcement Realities

The Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG), which entered into force on June 28, 2025, implements the European Accessibility Act (EAA) into German law [4, 12, 17]. This legislation marks the first time that private economic actors—specifically including online shop operators offering electronic commerce services to consumers—are legally required to ensure accessibility [2, 4]. The framework encompasses websites and mobile applications used for consumer contracts, mandating that digital interfaces remain accessible to individuals with disabilities, such as those facing visual impairments due to insufficient text and background color contrast [2, 4].

While the scope applies broadly to digital commerce, micro-enterprises providing services—defined as having fewer than ten employees and either an annual turnover or balance sheet total of no more than two million euros—are exempt from service-related accessibility requirements [4, 17]. However, this exemption does not apply if these micro-enterprises place physical products under the scope of the BFSG onto the market, as product compliance rules remain strictly binding [4].

A central point of friction in the enforcement reality involves the strict separation of institutional roles. The Bundesfachstelle Barrierefreiheit functions as an informational and advisory body, providing structured guidance, FAQs, and background knowledge regarding the BFSG framework [2, 12, 17]. It does not execute market surveillance or issue financial penalty notices. Instead, structured penalty systems and direct market monitoring are handled separately by designated market surveillance authorities [15]. Consequently, while the Bundesfachstelle focuses on dissemination and compliance support, actual enforcement actions and audits remain distinct administrative mechanisms managed elsewhere within the regulatory structure.
