Overview of the European Accessibility Act (EAA) Thresholds

Under the European Accessibility Act (EAA), which went into effect on June 28, 2025, microenterprises benefit from specific exemptions, though the scope is narrower than many assume [6, 7]. The legal definition of a microenterprise relies on EU Recommendation 2003/361/EC, which establishes a strict two-part criteria framework [7]. To qualify, a business must maintain an employee count of fewer than 10 employees [6, 7].

However, meeting the headcount restriction alone is insufficient. The enterprise must also satisfy financial thresholds where either the annual turnover or the annual balance sheet total does not exceed €2 million [6, 7]. Both conditions must be met simultaneously; a company failing to satisfy either metric does not qualify for the exemption [6, 7]. Furthermore, partner and linked enterprise rules apply, meaning the headcount and financials of parent or affiliated entities may be factored into the final calculation [7].

Crucially, the exemption itself possesses a major structural limitation regarding business models. Microenterprises that provide services are exempt from EAA accessibility requirements [6, 7]. Conversely, microenterprises that manufacture or sell physical products remain fully covered by the legislation and must comply with applicable standards such as EN 301 549 [6, 7].
