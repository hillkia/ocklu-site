The Data Gap: Conflicting and Incomplete Official Data

The current regulatory landscape regarding employee count thresholds in Indonesia is characterized by a significant disconnect between public perception and the actual text of recent legal instruments. A critical examination of the available research pool reveals that there is no definitive, up-to-date Ministry of Manpower regulation (Permenaker) that establishes a new, universal numerical threshold for employee counts for the 2024/2025 period. This absence creates a vacuum where outdated metrics or misinterpreted capital-based definitions are often mistakenly applied as headcount limits.

The most recent comprehensive source in the pool, Legalitas.org (Source [11]), explicitly highlights this confusion. It notes that current official data on precise employee count thresholds is conflicting or incomplete. Specifically, Source [11] clarifies that business scales are defined based on capital, not employee numbers, under the current framework. This finding directly contradicts the common assumption that a specific headcount limit (such as the previously cited 20-employee threshold for certain labor protections) remains the primary regulatory trigger for all business classifications in 2024/2025.

Furthermore, the research pool includes a reference to Permenaker No. 18 Tahun 2024 (Source [12]), but the provided excerpt is a raw PDF header and does not contain the substantive text defining employee thresholds. Consequently, it is impossible to verify from this source whether any new headcount-based regulations were introduced. Without a direct, current legal citation from a fully accessible and verified Permenaker text, any claim specifying a particular employee number as the current legal threshold is unsupported. The data gap is not merely a lack of information; it is a fundamental ambiguity in how business size is legally measured, shifting the focus from labor headcount to capital structure, a distinction that remains under-communicated in official guidance.
