California Pay Data Reporting and Employee Headcount Thresholds

Private employers operating in California face mandatory compliance obligations under Government Code Section 12999, as amended by Senate Bill 1162 (SB 1162) [5, 7]. Specifically, any private employer with a total headcount of 100 or more employees—provided at least one of those employees works within California—must submit an annual pay data report to the California Civil Rights Department (CRD) [5, 7]. This statutory threshold applies regardless of whether the organization is headquartered within the state, as long as the California employee nexus is met.

In addition to traditional direct hires, the regulatory framework imposes distinct headcount criteria concerning contingent labor. Private employers maintaining 100 or more workers hired through labor contractors (with at least one individual performing work in California) are legally required to file a separate, dedicated pay data report covering those contracted personnel [5]. This dual-reporting requirement ensures that organizations cannot circumvent transparency obligations by shifting portions of their workforce onto third-party vendor payrolls.

The annual submission must detail comprehensive workforce metrics categorized by gender, race, ethnicity, and specific job categories [5, 7]. Because the California Civil Rights Department applies an expansive interpretation to these statutory criteria, affected employers face substantial administrative burdens to accurately calculate and verify both direct employee and labor contractor headcounts ahead of annual deadlines [6, 7].
