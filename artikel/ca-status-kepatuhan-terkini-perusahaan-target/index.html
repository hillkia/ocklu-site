Operational Compliance: The 2025 Supplier Quality Shift

Target Corporation’s Supplier Performance Management (SPM) team is executing a specific 2025 rollout of updated quality standards that directly impacts domestic merchandise vendors [11]. This operational shift is not a broad regulatory mandate but a contractual enforcement mechanism designed to ensure that goods arrive when expected and without damage, thereby driving guest purchasability and shared growth [11]. The core of this update is the revised "Perfect Order Program," which now enforces three distinct metrics with strict financial penalties: ASN Availability, ASN Accuracy, and Physical Barcode Accuracy [11].

For each of these metrics, the compliance rate is set at $0.75 per carton, with a maximum penalty of $100 per instance, and a performance goal of 100% [11]. While the measurement method for ASN Availability remains unchanged—requiring an error-free EDI 856 Advance Ship Notice before the in-yard date—Target is adjusting the broader portfolio to include new accuracy checks [11]. Specifically, ASN Accuracy now scrutinizes the shipment or item level of the EDI 856, while Physical Barcode Accuracy introduces a new verification layer for physical labels [11]. These changes represent the current actionable compliance status for vendors, requiring immediate operational adjustments to avoid financial penalties.

It is critical to differentiate this operational compliance from broader regulatory reporting thresholds. The SPM rollout is a private, contractual obligation between Target and its suppliers, focused on logistics and data integrity [11]. In contrast, regulatory thresholds discussed in other contexts, such as the Swiss draft Federal Act on Sustainable Corporate Governance, apply to public disclosure based on employee headcount and revenue [5]. For instance, the Swiss proposal sets a reporting threshold at 1,000 employees and CHF 450 million in revenue, a scale and nature entirely distinct from Target’s vendor-specific quality metrics [5]. Similarly, while the SEC requires publicly traded companies to report on workforce-related metrics following 2020 amendments to Regulation S-K, these are statutory obligations for public entities, not operational quality gates for private suppliers [17]. The 2025 Target changes are therefore a matter of supply chain efficiency and contract enforcement, not statutory corporate governance or sustainability disclosure.
