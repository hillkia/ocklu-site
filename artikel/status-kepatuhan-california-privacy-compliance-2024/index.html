California Privacy Protection Agency Enforcement Overview

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) has actively stepped into its role as a leading data broker regulatory and enforcement agency [13]. Throughout 2024, the CPPA established baseline fine thresholds for CPRA violations reaching up to $2,500 per violation and $7,500 for intentional violations [5]. Because of California's massive consumer base, these statutory penalties can rapidly scale into millions of dollars [5].

Under the Delete Act, enforcement actions specifically targeted data brokers failing to meet annual registration requirements [12, 13]. The CPPA holds the explicit authority to fine non-compliant entities $200 per day for failing to register [13]. Leveraging this mechanism, the agency settled cases with multiple data brokers for a combined total of $226,400 in administrative fines [13]. Notable enforcement actions include penalties against specific entities for registration failures: Datamasters faced a $45,000 fine, S&P Global incurred a $62,600 penalty [12], and National Public Data faced a pursued administrative action for a $46,000 fine [13].

Furthermore, the CPPA entered into a notable stipulated final order with Background Alert, Inc., giving the company a stark choice: pay a $50,000 fine for being 250 days late on its registration or suspend its data broker operations entirely for three years [13]. Beyond these individual monetary penalties, 495 data brokers successfully registered with the CPPA, generating an estimated $3.3 million in registration fees to fund ongoing oversight [13].

Statutory Fine Thresholds and Corporate Liability

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) enforces statutory fine thresholds under the CPRA reaching up to $2,500 per unintentional violation and up to $7,500 per intentional violation [5, 22]. Inflation-adjusted frameworks also position these baseline penalties around $2,663 and $7,988 respectively [17].

Because these fines apply on a per-violation basis and businesses frequently process data belonging to millions of California consumers, penalties can rapidly escalate into the millions of dollars [5]. Furthermore, organizations face additional financial exposure beyond civil penalties, including statutory registration non-compliance fees such as $200 per day [13], alongside private rights of action for data breaches [17].
