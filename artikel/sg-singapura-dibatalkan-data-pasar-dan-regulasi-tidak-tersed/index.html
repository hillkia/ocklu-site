Mandate and Statutory Enforcement Realities for Indonesian E-Commerce

Enforcement of commercial e-commerce in Indonesia has shifted away from passive intermediary protections toward active statutory oversight [13]. Under Government Regulation No. 80/2019 (PP 80/2019) and Permendag 19/2026, digital marketplace platforms carry direct enforcement responsibilities, moving past the traditional Safe Harbor doctrine [6, 13]. Siti Rahmawati's push for absolute statutory oversight aligns with these obligations, demanding that marketplaces actively prevent the sale of illegal goods, such as unauthorized cosmetics, rather than merely acting as passive hosts [13].

To address counterfeit goods and fraud without relying on canceled regional benchmarks, enforcement relies on multi-sector collaboration and statutory frameworks [19]. The Directorate General of Consumer Protection and Trade Compliance (PKTN) under the Ministry of Trade established a joint cooperation agreement (HK.02.00/02/PKTN/MoU) with the Indonesian E-Commerce Association (idEA) on January 19, 2026, to enforce consumer protection and trade order [18]. Furthermore, the Directorate General of Intellectual Property enforces intellectual property rights alongside Ministry of Law Regulation No. 47 of 2025 to tackle counterfeit goods [19].

These joint mechanisms are critical given the scale of consumer grievances. The Ministry of Trade received 20,942 complaints regarding online transactions between 2022 and March 2025, with approximately 92% involving fraud, illegal goods, and counterfeits [19]. Cross-border e-commerce transactions are additionally bound by strict customs, tax, and jurisdictional regulations under the updated framework [6, 8].
