Introduction to ACM Enforcement and Statutory Frameworks

The Netherlands Authority for Consumers and Markets (ACM) operates as the primary competition and consumer protection regulator in the Netherlands, headquartered in The Hague [19]. Established on April 1, 2013, through the merger of the Consumer Authority, the Independent Postal and Telecommunications Authority (OPTA), and the Netherlands Competition Authority (NMa), the ACM functions under the responsibility of the Ministry of Economic Affairs [19]. Its statutory mandate encompasses competition oversight, sector-specific regulation, and the enforcement of consumer protection laws, including Section 24 of the Dutch Competition Act [19].

When the ACM suspects a violation, its enforcement officers may launch an investigation, recording their findings in a statement of objections handed over to the ACM’s Legal Department (JZ) [2]. The Legal Department advises the Board of ACM on whether to impose a fine and determines the appropriate amount [2]. Fines can reach up to 900,000 euros per violation or 10 percent of the affected turnover, with the final amount depending on the seriousness, duration, and specific circumstances of the case, while requiring proportionality [2]. Notable enforcement actions include a 41-million-euro fine imposed on Nederlandse Spoorwegen in 2017 for abuse of its position through predatory pricing [19]. Additionally, as of February 4, 2025, the ACM became the competent authority for supervising compliance with the Digital Services Act (DSA) in the Netherlands, granting it the competence to impose fines of up to 6% of worldwide annual turnover [14].

Administrative enforcement decisions and fine issuances are structurally bound by the rules of administrative law set out in the General Administrative Law Act (Algemene wet bestuursrecht, Awb) [17]. The Awb governs the relationship between public authorities, citizens, and businesses, imposing a duty on authorities to ensure transparency and maintain proportionality when making decisions [17]. Businesses and individuals subject to an ACM fining decision retain the right to file an objection or an appeal [2].
