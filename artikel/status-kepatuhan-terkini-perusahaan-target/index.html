The Regulatory Baseline: Defining the Employee Count Mandate

The regulatory concept of an "H-1B-dependent employer" is a specific classification used by the United States Department of Labor to identify companies where a significant fraction of the workforce consists of workers in H-1B status [5]. This designation was introduced by the American Competitiveness and Workforce Improvement Act (ACWIA) in 1998 and operationalized through the Interim Final H-1B Rule of December 20, 2000, with regulations codified in 20 CFR 655.736 [5]. The primary objective of this classification is to curtail the use of H-1B visas for replacing American skilled workers with cheaper foreign labor, striking a balance between preventing large-scale exploitation and minimizing regulatory burdens on employers who use the visa sparingly [5].

For employers classified as H-1B-dependent, specific additional attestations are required in the Labor Condition Application for any H-1B beneficiary offered an annual compensation of less than $60,000 and without a master's degree [5]. Furthermore, these employers have been targeted for additional H-1B fees under Public Law 111-230 and 114–113 [5]. It is crucial to note that this dependence status does not apply to Labor Condition Applications filed for H-1B1 and E-3 workers [5].
