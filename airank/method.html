Method, in full

Anyone should be able to repeat this and get the same shape of answer. If you cannot reproduce a number here, it is our mistake and we want to hear about it.

1. The questions

Each board starts from questions a buyer actually types, generated per category and never containing a brand name. Brand-name questions only test recall, not recommendation, so they are excluded from the score (a single control question is used on brand reports and marked as such).

2. The engines

When the primary engine is unavailable the call falls to a backup and the row is flagged turun_kelas (downgraded). A downgraded answer is still real data, but it is not the same measurement, so it is labelled rather than blended.

3. What is extracted

From each raw answer: whether the brand is named, its ordinal position in the list, whether its domain is cited, which competitor domains appear, and the tone of the sentence around the mention. Extraction is deterministic string work on the stored text — no model is asked to judge another model's output.

4. The score

visibility% = named in N of M unbranded questions. share of voice = your mentions over all brand mentions in the same answers. AIRANK score = 0.6 × visibility + 0.25 × share of voice + a position bonus (up to 15 for ranking first) + 5 if the brand is recognised when asked directly.

5. What this cannot tell you

Absence is not proof. Not being named in these calls does not mean a brand is never named. Answer engines vary by user, location, session and day.

Failed calls are failures, not zeros. When every engine is down, the row is recorded as an error and excluded. It is never counted as "not mentioned" — that would manufacture bad news out of our own outage.

Sample sizes are small and printed. Every board states how many live calls it is built from. A board with fewer than three successful calls is not published at all.

6. Raw data

Every board publishes its underlying evidence as JSON at /airank/data/<board>.json — the prompt, the engine, the timestamp and the full answer text. Cite it, check it, or argue with it.
